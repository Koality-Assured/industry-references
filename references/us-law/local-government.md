# Local government law

This capture is a method, not a national ordinance code. It is not legal advice.

## What is not in this family

There is no official United States municipal code. State statute sites and court docket sites do not contain every city ordinance. County counts and city lists on a state page are included only when that official page stated them.

Commercial hosts (Municode, American Legal Publishing, eCode360, and similar) republish ordinances under contract with a city. They are `unofficial` unless the city's own site identifies that host as its code publisher. Confirm the city's identity before using the host. A search hit for the wrong city is a wrong law.

## Lookup

1. Name the municipality or county. A state page is the wrong corpus for a zoning, noise, or business-license ordinance.
2. Open the city or county's official website and find its charter and code link.
3. Record the publisher, the supplement date printed on the code, and whether the city posts ordinances adopted after that supplement.
4. If the official site has no code, say the ordinance was not located. Do not substitute a commercial search result that does not name that city.
5. Use the state court docket on the state page for cases. A docket is not the ordinance.

## Oklahoma example

State dockets and statutes are on the Oklahoma page (`states/ok.md`), including OSCN. OSCN district-court search does not replace a city code. For a Norman, Tulsa, or Oklahoma City ordinance, start from that city's official site.

## Court rules are not ordinances

Local rules of a trial court govern practice in that court. They are issued by the court, not the city council. Use the court-rules link on the state or federal page.
