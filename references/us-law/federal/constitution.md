# United States Constitution

This capture is a point-in-time locator and structural summary for attorney review, not legal advice and not a filing.

## Purpose

The National Archives presents the Constitution as the charter that replaced the Articles of Confederation and vested the union in the people. The parchment is on permanent display at the Archives. This page records where that official presentation and its transcript live. It does not restate the articles or amendments.

## Structural summary

The overview page separates three layers and links a transcript rather than embedding the full instrument:

- The Constitution proper, with a transcript page.
- Amendments 1–10, which the Archives identifies as the Bill of Rights.
- Amendments 11–27, described on the page as later changes and additions.

The transcript page fetched here includes the early articles (Article I through Article IV appear before the saved extract was cut). A complete article-and-amendment inventory still requires opening the transcript. Do not treat a commentary site as a substitute while constitution.congress.gov is blocked.

The Administrative Office’s court-structure page states that the U.S. courts were created under Article III to administer justice. That is a pointer to the judicial branch, not a restatement of Article III.
