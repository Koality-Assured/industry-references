# Inferior courts, rules, and dockets

This capture is a point-in-time locator and structural summary for attorney review, not legal advice and not a filing.

## Purpose

Below the Supreme Court, the federal judiciary is the regional courts of appeals, the district courts, the bankruptcy courts, and a small set of nationwide courts. National rules of practice sit on uscourts.gov. Case files sit in PACER, which charges fees. This page names those corpora and what each one governs. It does not reprint a rule.

## Structural summary

### Courts

The appeals page describes twelve regional circuits, shown on the circuit map, plus a thirteenth court, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Federal Circuit, with nationwide jurisdiction in specialized cases such as patent cases and appeals from the Court of International Trade and the Court of Federal Claims. Circuit judges usually sit in panels of three. The courts of appeals also review some federal agency decisions.

District courts are the primary trial courts. They try cases, find facts, and apply law. Within limits set by Congress and the Constitution, they hear nearly all categories of federal civil and criminal cases.

Bankruptcy courts are units of the district courts. The bankruptcy page says federal courts have exclusive jurisdiction over bankruptcy cases, that there are 90 bankruptcy courts, and that those courts hear matters referred by the district courts. The applicable statute is the Bankruptcy Code, Title 11.

The Court of International Trade’s site says it has nationwide jurisdiction over civil actions arising out of the customs and international trade laws. The Court of Federal Claims site identifies that court; the appeals page is what places its appeals in the Federal Circuit. The Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces states that it exercises worldwide appellate jurisdiction over members of the armed forces on active duty and other persons subject to the Uniform Code of Military Justice, with five civilian judges appointed for 15-year terms, and that its decisions are subject to direct review by the Supreme Court.

### Sentencing guidelines

The U.S. Sentencing Commission is an independent agency in the judicial branch, created in the Sentencing Reform Act of 1984. It promulgates guidelines that federal judges consult when sentencing individuals, and it publishes a Guidelines Manual when amendments take effect. The page’s current manual is the 2025 Guidelines Manual, incorporating amendments effective November 1, 2025. The guidelines are not the criminal code and not the rules of criminal procedure.

### Local rules

Local rules are prescribed by district courts and courts of appeals and must be consistent with acts of Congress and the national rules. The current-rules page says a court’s authority is 28 U.S.C. §§ 2071(a)–(b) and the local-rules provisions of the appellate, bankruptcy, civil, and criminal rules. Section 205 of the E-Government Act of 2002 requires courts to post local rules on their own websites. The same page tells readers to use the Federal Court Finder for the list of court sites. The directory fetched here is [Court Website Links](https://www.uscourts.gov/about-federal-courts/federal-courts-public/court-website-links). `https://www.uscourts.gov/court-locator` returned HTTP 403 and was not used.

### Dockets

PACER (Public Access to Court Electronic Records) is the judiciary’s electronic public-access service for federal court records. It is fee-based. The [pricing page](https://pacer.uscourts.gov/pacer-pricing-how-fees-work) states $0.10 per page, a cap of $3 for a single document, no cap for search-result pages, non-case reports, or transcripts, and $2.40 for an audio file. Fees are billed quarterly and waived if charges for the quarter are $30 or less. Court opinions are free. Parties can receive one free copy of a notice of electronic filing. The homepage also announced a temporary fee increase effective January 1, 2027; the dollar amount of that increase was not on the pricing page captured here. This capture did not open any docket.

## Matter map

| Class | Start |
| --- | --- |
| Criminal | District court, criminal rules, Title 18, then the guidelines at sentencing. Appeal to the regional circuit, or the Armed Forces court for persons under the UCMJ. |
| Civil | District court and the civil rules. Evidence rules at trial. Appeal to the regional circuit, the Federal Circuit, or the Court of International Trade’s own docket when the case is a customs or trade action. |
| Administrative | Often a court of appeals reviewing agency action, or the Court of Federal Claims when that court’s jurisdiction is the claim. |
| Bankruptcy | Bankruptcy court under Title 11 and the bankruptcy rules. |
