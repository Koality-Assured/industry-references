# Tribal law boundary

Federally recognized tribes are separate governments. State statute pages and federal titles do not contain a tribe's code or a tribal court's rules.

This family does not catalog tribal codes, tribal constitutions, or tribal court opinions. Those texts live with the tribe. Before answering a tribal-law question, ask which tribe, then use that tribe's official publisher. If this session has not fetched it, the answer is unverified.

Federal Indian-law statutes and Supreme Court opinions are federal law about the relationship among tribes, the United States, and states. They are not a substitute for the tribe's own code. Start federal questions from [`federal/`](./federal/). Do not treat a Bureau of Indian Affairs page as the tribe's legislature.
